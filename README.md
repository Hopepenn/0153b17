## 1. 【The LEGO Assembly Line】

**What type of bond links amino acids together to form a protein? Which amino acids form hydrogen bonds with each other?**

### Answer:

#### 1. The Bond Linking Amino Acids
Amino acids are linked together by **peptide bonds** to form polypeptide chains and proteins. A peptide bond is a covalent amide bond formed via a condensation (dehydration) reaction, where the carboxyl group (-COOH) of one amino acid reacts with the amino group (-NH₂) of the next, releasing a molecule of water (H₂O).

#### 2. Hydrogen Bonds in PDB 3KMD (p53 Core Domain and DNA Complex)
PDB ID `3KMD` represents the crystal structure of the p53 tumor suppressor core domain bound to DNA as a tetramer. Hydrogen bonding in this structure occurs across two main contexts: protein backbone stabilization and specific amino acid-to-amino acid/DNA interactions.

##### A. Backbone Hydrogen Bonds (Secondary Structure)
Throughout the p53 core domain monomers in 3KMD, hydrogen bonds form between the atoms of the polypeptide backbone:
- **The Donors and Acceptors:** The hydrogen atom attached to the backbone nitrogen (N-H) acts as a hydrogen bond donor, while the carbonyl oxygen (C=O) acts as an acceptor.
- **Structures Formed:** These backbone hydrogen bonds stabilize the $\alpha$-helices and $\beta$-sheets that make up the core scaffolding of the p53 protein.

##### B. Side-Chain and Protein-DNA Hydrogen Bonds
When looking at how amino acid side chains form hydrogen bonds with each other or with the DNA in 3KMD, specific polar and charged residues play critical roles:
- **Polar/Charged Amino Acids:** Residues like Arginine (Arg), Lysine (Lys), Glutamine (Gln), Asparagine (Asn), Serine (Ser), Threonine (Thr), and Tyrosine (Tyr) feature side chains rich in nitrogen, oxygen, and hydrogen atoms capable of acting as hydrogen bond donors or acceptors.
- **Intra- and Inter-molecular Networks:** Within the protein core and at subunit interfaces, these side chains hydrogen bond with each other or with water molecules to maintain the tertiary fold and the tetrameric assembly.
- **DNA Recognition:** In 3KMD, specific amino acids (particularly Arginine and Lysine) extend into the grooves of the DNA double helix, forming precise hydrogen bonds and electrostatic interactions with the DNA bases and phosphate backbone to sequence-specifically bind target genes.

## 2. 【The Double-Track Highway of Life’s Blueprint】

**How do nucleotides assemble to form double-stranded DNA? What are the basic structural components of DNA? Is DNA right-handed or left-handed? List the names of the four DNA bases. Which bases form hydrogen bonds with each other?**

### Answer:

#### 1. Basic Structural Components of DNA
A DNA molecule is a polymer made of repeating units called nucleotides. Each individual nucleotide consists of three core components:
- **A 5-Carbon Sugar:** Specifically, deoxyribose (which lacks one oxygen atom compared to the ribose found in RNA).
- **A Phosphate Group:** Attached to the 5' carbon of the sugar.
- **A Nitrogenous Base:** Attached to the 1' carbon of the sugar.

#### 2. How Nucleotides Assemble to Form Double-Stranded DNA
- **Forming a Single Strand (Backbone):** Nucleotides join together through phosphodiester bonds. The phosphate group of one nucleotide connects to the 3' carbon of the sugar of the next nucleotide, creating a strong sugar-phosphate backbone ($5' \rightarrow 3'$ direction).
- **Forming the Double Strand:** Two single polynucleotide strands align antiparallel to one another (meaning one runs $5' \rightarrow 3'$ and the other runs $3' \rightarrow 5'$).
- **The Double Helix:** The two strands twist around each other into a double helix. This structure is stabilized by two main forces:
  - Hydrogen bonds forming between the nitrogenous bases pointing inward.
  - Base-stacking interactions (hydrophobic effects and $\pi-\pi$ stacking) between adjacent flat base pairs along the vertical axis of the helix.

#### 3. Handedness of DNA
Standard cellular DNA (known as B-form DNA) is **right-handed**. As the double helix twists upward, the strands curve clockwise away from the central axis. *(Note: While rare left-handed conformations like Z-DNA exist under specific cellular conditions, B-DNA is the standard physiological form).*

#### 4. The Four DNA Bases
The four nitrogenous bases found in DNA are:
- **Adenine (A)**
- **Thymine (T)**
- **Cytosine (C)**
- **Guanine (G)**

#### 5. Base Pairing and Hydrogen Bonds
The bases pair up across the center of the double helix according to Chargaff's rules (complementary base pairing):
- **Adenine (A)** always pairs with **Thymine (T)**, held together by **2 hydrogen bonds**.
- **Cytosine (C)** always pairs with **Guanine (G)**, held together by **3 hydrogen bonds** (making the G-C pair structurally more stable than the A-T pair).
